The rules, and how Pulse keeps them

Allowed

Personal apps, non-commercial apps, learning projects.

Not allowed

Selling the app, ads, subscriptions or any paid use, unless you buy their paid plan.

Must do

Show a credit to AscendAPI. Be gentle: the free version has strict limits on how fast you ask.

  • Credit: “Exercise data and animations: ExerciseDB by AscendAPI” under every animation and in Settings, Training, Sources.
  • Gentle: one request at a time, at least 1.2 seconds apart. If ExerciseDB says “too many”, Pulse waits and tries later by itself.
  • Safety lock: the build stops with a clear message if anyone tries to make a Play Store version while the free ExerciseDB is switched on. So it can't be published by mistake.

Build steps

1Data

New tables for ExerciseDB data, muscle and equipment lists mapped to Pulse's, the 30-day check.

2Gentle client

One request at a time, 1.2 s apart, waits when told “too many”, picks up where it stopped.

3Merge

Join with your present exercises, keep history, ask you once about the 65 close names.

4Animations

Saving and playing, with pause, slow and full screen. The fallback order above.

5Screens

Exercise page, search and filters, workout cards, how-to sheet, settings, credits.

6Test and check

Unit tests, device tests, emulator walk-through, airplane mode, slow network, full phone.

How I'll test it

  • Rules in code: name matching, muscle and equipment mapping, the fallback order, the wait-and-retry rules.
  • A fake ExerciseDB on the computer that answers slowly, says “too many”, or breaks halfway, to check Pulse waits and resumes without losing anything.
  • On the emulator: first start, airplane mode, slow network, phone storage nearly full, app killed in the middle of downloading.
  • Your history: a copy of real workouts goes through the merge, and every workout and record must still be there.
