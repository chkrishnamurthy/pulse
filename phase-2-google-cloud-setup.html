Phase 2 · Google Cloud setup · checked 22 Nov 2026

Open the door to your Fitbit data, and read your first steps

In this phase you set up a free Google Cloud project, turn on the Google Health API, create a sign-in for your app, and then read your real Fitbit data in Google's OAuth Playground website. You don't write any code yet. Follow the steps in order and tick each one off as you go.

What you'll have at the end

A working key to your own health data

  • A Google Cloud project named fitbit-track-dev
  • The Google Health API turned on
  • A sign-in screen with you added as a test user
  • Your first real reply from Google: your steps
  • Time needed: about 45 minutes. Cost: free.
The map

Eight steps, in two places

Steps 0 to 6 happen on your phone and in the Google Cloud Console. Step 7 happens in the OAuth Playground, a Google website that lets you try the API with no code.

Good news first · your device

Your Fitbit Air is supported

Google's device list for the Health API names the Fitbit Air and says which data types it sends. This replaces the "◐ depends on your model" marks from Phase 1.

So we skip the ECG permission. For heart-rate zones we can still work them out ourselves from your heart-rate data. Weight, water and food are not sensor data; they show up only if you log them yourself.

What this means for permissions: you need 5 read-only scopes (Step 5). That covers every metric you asked for, including SpO2, sleep stages and active zone minutes.

One choice to know about

"Testing" or "In production"?

Google gives every new app a publishing status. It only changes how long you stay signed in. Both are free and both are limited to 100 users.

Use now, in Phase 2

Testing

This is the default, and it's what Google's own codelab uses. Only the test users you add can sign in. The catch is that your sign-in (the "refresh token") runs out after 7 days. For today's test that doesn't matter.

Switch later, before Phase 5 (sync)

In production

Your sign-in usually lasts until you revoke it or don't use it for about 6 months. Google recommends this so users aren't logged out every week. The app is still "unverified" (fine for 1 person), so you may see a warning screen when you sign in. It's your own app, so that's expected.

Do it · step by step

The click-by-click guide

Words in grey boxes are the exact labels you'll see on screen. You can use two different Google accounts. Your builder account owns the Cloud project (Steps 1–6). Your Fitbit account is the one linked to your Fitbit Air. It must be added as a test user (Step 4) and used to sign in (Step 7). They can be the same account, but they don't have to be.

0

Link Google Health to your Google account (on your phone)

Why: the API can only find your data if your Google Health profile is linked to your Google account. If it isn't, every call fails with ACCOUNT_NOT_LINKED.

  1. Open the Google Health app (the app your Fitbit Air syncs with).
  2. If it asks, tap Sign in with Google and pick your Google account.
  3. If you had an old Fitbit account, follow the app's prompts to move it over (Google's help link is below).
  4. Open the app and let your Fitbit Air sync once, so fresh data is waiting.
✔ Done when the app shows your Google account and today's steps.

Docs: Setup → "Link Google Health before OAuth consent" · Fitbit account migration help

1

Create a Google Cloud project

Why: a project is a free folder in Google Cloud that holds your app's settings and keys. Google suggests a separate project for testing, so we put -dev in the name.

  1. Go to console.cloud.google.com and sign in. Accept the terms if this is your first visit.
  2. At the top, click the project picker Select a project, then New Project.
  3. Project name: fitbit-track-dev. Location: leave it as No organization.
  4. Click Create. Wait a few seconds, then pick the new project in the project picker.
✔ Done when the top bar shows fitbit-track-dev. You don't need a credit card.

Docs: Codelab: first API call

2

Turn on the Google Health API

Why: APIs start switched off. This tells Google that your project wants to use this one.

  1. Open the menu (☰) → APIs & Services → Library.
  2. Search for Google Health API and click it.
  3. Click Enable.

Shortcut: open the Google Health API page directly (check that your project is selected at the top).

✔ Done when the page shows API Enabled (or a Manage button).

Docs: Setup → manual setup

3

Set up the sign-in screen (Google Auth Platform)

Why: this is the screen you'll see when your app asks, "Can this app read your health data?"

  1. Go to APIs & Services → Credentials → + Create Credentials → OAuth client ID.
  2. Click Configure consent screen. If you see "Google Auth Platform not configured yet", click Get Started.
  3. App information: App name = Fitbit Track. User support email = your Gmail. Click Next.
  4. Audience: choose External, then Next. (Internal is only for company Google Workspace accounts.)
  5. Contact Information: your Gmail, then Next.
  6. Finish: tick the box agreeing to the Google API Services User Data Policy, then click Create.
✔ Done when you see the Google Auth Platform overview with Branding, Audience, Clients and Data Access in the left menu.
4

Add yourself as a test user

Why: in Testing mode, only people on this list can sign in. Without it you get "access blocked".

  1. In the left menu click Audience.
  2. Check that Publishing status says Testing and User type says External.
  3. Under Test users click + Add users, type your Gmail address (the one your Fitbit uses), then click Save.
✔ Done when your address appears in the Test users list.

Shortcut: Audience page

5

Choose what data the app may read (scopes)

Why: each "scope" is a permission for one group of data. We only ask to read, never to change, and only for the groups we need.

  1. In the left menu click Data Access → Add or remove scopes.
  2. In the filter box, search for Google Health API.
  3. Tick these 5 lines. Each one ends in .readonly:
Scope (full text)What it covers for you
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/googlehealth.activity_and_fitness.readonlySteps, distance, floors, calories, active zone minutes, workouts, VO2 max
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/googlehealth.health_metrics_and_measurements.readonlyHeart rate, resting HR, HRV, SpO2, breathing rate, skin temperature
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/googlehealth.sleep.readonlySleep and sleep stages
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/googlehealth.profile.readonlyYour profile (for example height, used for reports)
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/googlehealth.settings.readonlyYour settings (for example units)
  1. Click Update, then Save at the bottom of the page.
Optional, skip for now…irn.readonly (irregular rhythm alerts, which your Air supports) and …location.readonly (GPS routes of workouts). We can add them later. Don't add any .writeonly scopes.
✔ Done when the 5 scopes are listed under "Your sensitive scopes" or "Your restricted scopes".

Docs: Scopes · Shortcut: Data Access page

6

Create the OAuth client (your app's ID card)

Why: the client ID tells Google which app is asking. This first client is a Web application type, used only for testing in the Playground. We'll make a separate Android client in Phase 3.

  1. In the left menu click Clients → + Create client. (Or use APIs & Services → Credentials → + Create Credentials → OAuth client ID.)
  2. Application type: Web application. Name: Playground test.
  3. Leave Authorized JavaScript origins empty.
  4. Under Authorized redirect URIs click + Add URI twice and add:
Redirect URI 1
https://developers.google.com/oauthplayground
Redirect URI 2
https://www.google.com
  1. Click Create. A box shows your Client ID and Client secret.
  2. Right away, click Download JSON. Google says you can't see the secret again later.
Keep the secret secretSave the JSON file somewhere private (for example your Documents folder), not in D:\fitbit-track, not in chat, and never inside the Android app. Don't paste it to me. If it leaks, open the client and use + Add secret to make a new one, then delete the old one.
✔ Done when you have the JSON file saved and the client appears under Clients.

Docs: Setup → "Create a project and an OAuth client" · Shortcut: Credentials page

7

Make your first real call in the OAuth Playground

Why: this proves the whole chain works (project, permissions, sign-in, your linked data) before we write any Android code.

7a · Connect the Playground to your client

  1. Open developers.google.com/oauthplayground.
  2. Click the gear icon (OAuth 2.0 Configuration, top right).
  3. Tick Use your own OAuth credentials. Paste your OAuth Client ID and OAuth Client secret from the JSON file. Close the box.

7b · Sign in and get a token

  1. In Step 1 Select & authorize APIs, paste the 5 scopes, separated by spaces, into the Input your own scopes box at the bottom:
5 scopes, one line
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/googlehealth.activity_and_fitness.readonly https://www.googleapis.com/auth/googlehealth.health_metrics_and_measurements.readonly https://www.googleapis.com/auth/googlehealth.sleep.readonly https://www.googleapis.com/auth/googlehealth.profile.readonly https://www.googleapis.com/auth/googlehealth.settings.readonly
  1. Click Authorize APIs. Sign in with your Fitbit account (the test user from Step 4), even if the Cloud project belongs to a different account. If Google shows a warning that the app isn't verified, that's your own app: continue. Tick all the permissions and click Continue.
  2. Back in the Playground, Step 2 Exchange authorization code for tokens opens. Click that button.
  3. You now have an access token (it lasts about 1 hour, expires_in: 3599) and a refresh token (about 7 days in Testing, refresh_token_expires_in: 604799).

7c · Ask for your data

In Step 3 Configure request to API, set the HTTP Method and Request URI as below, then click Send the request. Try them in this order.

401 UNAUTHENTICATEDThe access token ran out (after ~1 hour)In the Playground Step 2 click Refresh access token
400 INVALID_ARGUMENTA typo in the filter or body, or a short data source nameCopy the request again from this page; use the coded filter version
