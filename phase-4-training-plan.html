Adding Boostcamp-style training to Pulse

What is known about Boostcamp's private API, how to connect to it safely, and a step-by-step plan to build plans, a schedule and workout logging inside your own app.

Written 24 November 2026 for the Pulse Android app (Kotlin, Compose, Room). I read your code, the code or notes of eight community projects, Boostcamp's terms and public pages. I did not sign in to Boostcamp, call its API or take its app apart.

The short answer

  • Build the training features in Pulse, on your own database. Do not make Pulse a screen on top of Boostcamp.
  • Use Boostcamp once, as a source of your past data. You save your own history file in your browser; Pulse reads the file.
  • The private API could not run these features anyway. None of the eight projects I read has a way to log a workout or change a schedule through it.
  • First step: a second database file. Pulse's present database erases everything when its layout changes and when you sign out.

Read this first: Boostcamp's terms do not allow a live connection

  • Section 8 bans reverse engineering, and bans manual or automated tools that crawl or scrape. It makes no exception for your own account. Pro does not change this.
  • Section 8 also bans using the platform to “develop a competing Boostcamp Platform or service”. A private tool just for you is a different matter from an app you publish, but it is still their rule to apply. This is not legal advice.
  • Section 17 lets them close an account at once, without notice. The real risk for you is losing the Pro account and its history.
  • Coach programs belong to Boostcamp and the coaches. Do not copy them into Pulse. Bring in only your own logs and programs you wrote.

Best next move: write to support@boostcamp.app, ask for an export of your own data and ask if personal API use is allowed. Saving your own history by hand is the lowest-risk way, but the terms do not clearly allow it either, so it is your choice.

boostcamp-api

Python, by Alex Keyes. Read-only in practice. Not on PyPI. It renews the token only on error 403, not 401, with no lock against two renewals at once. It stores the session in a pickle file, which is unsafe to load if someone changes it.

Good as: a map of paths. Not as: something to build on.

boostcamp-mcp

The history tool fails on every call in the main branch I read (a missing await; open fix, pull request 7). Once fixed, it still shows planned numbers instead of the ones you lifted (issue 8): one session read 570 kg instead of 3,750 kg.

Best part: its token renewer, with a lock and a 60-second safety margin.

n8n-nodes-boostcamp

TypeScript, MIT licence. The only one that writes programs and custom exercises. Its set reader guesses pounds or kilos from how heavy the numbers look. Do not copy that guess.

Also useful: garmin-boostcamp-sync for its field notes and boostcamp-exporter for getting the file.

2. Three ways to connect, and the one to choose

The key question: when Boostcamp changes something, what stops working in your app?

Also use the allowed road: Health Connect

Boostcamp officially saves finished workouts (date and duration) and bodyweight to Health Connect. It does not save sets, reps, weights or RPE there. So Health Connect can tell Pulse “you trained on Tuesday for 62 minutes”, but not what you lifted. Pulse already reads Health Connect, so this is a small addition.

Leave a door open for B

If Boostcamp gives you permission, B becomes one more class behind the same importer. Nothing else changes. Until then do not build it: no sign-in screen, no token store, no timer.

Four things in Pulse today that would lose or mix up training data

  • data/local/PulseDatabase.kt uses fallbackToDestructiveMigration(dropAllTables = true). Adding one table there would erase every logged set at the next layout change.
  • data/HealthRepositoryImpl.kt calls db.clearAllTables(), and sign-out runs it. Your lifting log should outlive a Google sign-out, or at least ask.
  • The table name workout is taken, and replaceWorkoutsFrom() deletes its rows on each sync. Never put gym logs in it.
  • Phone backup is switched off for all files. That is right for privacy, but it means a lost phone loses your log, so an export file is required.

3. The data model

The one rule that prevents most bugs: the plan, the calendar and what really happened are three separate things.

IDs

Every row gets its own random ID made in Pulse. Boostcamp IDs live only in import_ref (source, kind, outside ID, Pulse ID, content hash). Importing the same file twice changes nothing.

Weights

Save whole grams, plus exactly what you typed (“225”, lb). Grams make the math safe; the typed value makes the screen show 225 and never 224.99.

Dates

Planned days are plain dates with no time. Sessions keep exact start and end times, the time zone and the local day. A workout past midnight stays on the day it began.

4. How the three main features work

Plans: every edit asks “how far?”

Your choiceWhat Pulse doesPast workoutsOther weeks
Just todayChanges the open session onlySameSame
From now onNew revision; only future planned sessions move to itSameFuture ones change
The whole planNew revision; used the next time you start the plan tooSameFuture ones change

Schedule: order and date are separate

Live workout: saved at every tap

One set row

Last time · target · weight · reps · RPE · tick. Kinds: warm-up, working, drop, AMRAP, failure, timed. RPE 6 to 10 in half steps, optional, with “reps left” shown beside it.

Next weight (simple rules)

Fixed step; double progression (fill the rep range, then add weight); percent of training max; RPE based. Each is a plain function with tests, and the screen says why it suggests a weight. You can always change it.

Progress

Estimated max with Epley: weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30), only for 1 to 12 reps. Best weight, best reps at a weight, weekly sets per muscle, volume. Warm-ups and skipped sets never count.

5. Build order

Each phase ends with something you can use. Logging comes before the builder because plans are only worth making once you can follow them. Sizes are my estimate: S is about 1–2 focused days, M 3–5, L 6–10.

Phase 0S

Safe storage

  • New training/ package, TrainingDatabase in its own file, exportSchema = true, no destructive fallback
  • Wire it in PulseGraph.kt
  • Export and restore one JSON file you choose; sign-out asks about training data separately

Done when

A migration test passes. Export, uninstall, install, restore gives the same rows. A Google sign-out leaves training data alone.

Phase 1S–M

Exercises

  • Start from free-exercise-db: 876 exercises, Unlicense (check the pictures' origin before using them)
  • Search, filter, favourites; custom exercises with muscles, equipment, tracking type and rest
  • Archive, never delete, once used

Done when

You can add “Cable row, close grip”, find it by typing “row”, and rename it without changing past logs.

Phase 2L

Live logging

  • Empty workout, sets, supersets, swap exercise, notes
  • Rest timer, notification, plate calculator, “last time” column
  • Live heart rate from direct/LiveHeart.kt during the session

Done when

Kill the app mid-set and mid-rest, restart the phone, use it in flight mode: no set is lost and the timer is right.

Phase 3L

Plan builder

  • Weeks, days, exercises, set targets; copy week, copy day, drag to reorder
  • Deload week, progression rule per exercise
  • Revisions and the “how far?” question

Done when

Build a 12-week, 4-day plan in under ten minutes. Edit week 5 while in week 4: weeks 1–4 history is byte-for-byte the same.

Phase 4M

Schedule

  • Planned workouts in CalendarScreen.kt and on Today
  • Move, shift, swap, skip, pause, undo
  • Workout reminder through the present reminders/ code

Done when

Schedule rules are pure functions with tests for pins, pauses, month ends, clock changes and a missed week.

Phase 5M

Progress

  • Exercise page, records, muscle sets, week streak
  • Next-weight suggestions
  • New cards in Trends; “How is my bench?” in Ask Pulse

Done when

Editing an old set updates its records. Each suggestion can show its reason in one sentence.

Phase 6M

Boostcamp import

  • Pick a file → check → match exercises → preview → save in one go
  • One known set to prove kg or lb
  • Undo a whole import; keep the original file for a re-read

Done when

Counts match: workouts, sets and total weight in the file equal those in Pulse, with every skipped row listed. A second import adds zero rows.

Phase 7S–M

Health Connect and extras

  • Write finished sessions; read bodyweight
  • Join duplicates from Google, Boostcamp and Pulse
  • Next-workout widget

Done when

One gym visit shows as one workout, wherever it was recorded. The permission screen text says Pulse now also writes.

6. Getting your history out, once

No official export exists yet; search results show Boostcamp saying on Google Play that it is wanted. If support cannot help, this is the manual way other users take.

7. Risks and traps

Data traps: most of the real bugs are here

SupersetsThe outer record has no sets; the exercises are in supersets[].Unpack and keep a group ID. If not, whole exercises vanish.
Timed setsWith target_type: "time", the reps field holds seconds.Choose the column by type. A 60-second plank is not 60 reps.
Empty, zero, skippedZero can mean bodyweight, not entered or skipped. RPE 0 means none.Use null for unknown. Keep skipped. Bodyweight and assisted lifts get their own load type.
Numbers as textFields switch between text and numbers.Read both. Unknown fields are ignored; missing key fields stop that row and are listed.
Musclesmuscles_list appears in three shapes.Use the matched Pulse exercise's muscles instead.

App traps

Same workout three times

A gym visit can arrive from Pulse, Boostcamp through Health Connect, and Google. Give each written record a fixed clientRecordId, ignore records Pulse wrote, and join sessions that overlap by most of their time. Ask when unsure.

Health Connect permission

Pulse says “read-only” in its manifest notes and permission screen. Writing sessions changes that promise, so update the text. Also, LocalLane.turnOff() revokes all permissions at sign-out, the new ones included.

Rest alarm late

Exact alarms may be refused on Android 14 and newer. Let the foreground service do the timing from the saved end time, and use an exact alarm only when allowed. Test with the screen off for five minutes.

8. Decisions for you

  1. Will Pulse stay private? If you may publish it, skip the Boostcamp import, or get their written permission first.
  2. Email support before Phase 6? I suggest yes. Phases 0 to 5 need nothing from Boostcamp, so there is no waiting.
  3. How many tabs? Pulse has five already. I suggest a Train card on Today that opens a Train screen, with no sixth tab.
