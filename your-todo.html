Do it

Step by step

1

Check the Google Health app uses your Fitbit account

  1. On your phone, open the Google Health (Fitbit) app.
  2. Tap your profile picture. It should show your Fitbit Gmail.
  3. If it asks, tap Sign in with Google and pick the Fitbit account.
  4. Let your Fitbit Air sync once.
  5. Check your Android version: Settings → About phone. Pulse needs Android 9 or newer.
✔ Done when the app shows your Fitbit account and today's steps.
2

Create the Android sign-in (Google Cloud Console)

Why: Google lets the app ask for your data only if it knows the app's name and its digital fingerprint. No secret is needed, and nothing secret goes into the app.

  1. On your computer, sign in to Cloud Console with your current (builder) account and open Clients for fitbit-tracker-515502.
  2. Click + Create client.
  3. Application type: Android. Name: Pulse Android.
  4. Package name: paste this:
Package name
com.fitbittrack.pulse
  1. SHA-1 certificate fingerprint: paste this. It's the fingerprint of this computer's build key, which signed your app file.
SHA-1 fingerprint
0F:6F:83:92:E2:FD:99:DE:9D:FF:92:F7:FC:E6:47:7E:E3:9A:18:F7
  1. Click Create. There's no secret to download this time.
✔ Done when Pulse Android appears in the Clients list with the type Android.
3

Install the app on your phone

You don't need Android Studio. The app is one file, about 40 MB, already built on this computer:

App file
D:\fitbit-track\android\app\build\outputs\apk\debug\app-debug.apk
  1. Copy the file to your phone. Either use a USB cable (phone in "File transfer" mode, put the file in Downloads), or upload it to your own Google Drive and open it on the phone.
  2. On the phone, open Files → Downloads → tap app-debug.apk.
  3. If Android blocks installing unknown apps, tap Settings → turn on Allow from this source → go back.
  4. Tap Install. If Play Protect warns you, tap More details → Install anyway. It's your own app.
✔ Done when the Pulse icon is on your phone. Until you sign in, it shows sample data with a "Sample data" label.
4

Sign in, then switch to "In production"

  1. Open Pulse → tap Connect.
  2. Google asks which account to use: pick your Fitbit account.
  3. If you see "Google hasn't verified this app", tap Continue.
  4. Tick all permission boxes → Continue.
  5. Pulse checks your account and starts downloading your history. The first download takes a few minutes, and you can watch its progress on the Sync screen.

Then, so you aren't signed out every 7 days:

  1. On your computer, open Audience → click Publish app → Confirm.
  2. Publishing status now says In production. The app is still private: it's limited to 100 users and nobody else knows it exists.
  3. In Pulse, sign out and Connect once more, so you get the long-lasting sign-in.
✔ Done when Pulse shows your real steps, heart rate and sleep, and Audience says In production.
If the app shows an error

What the message means

Pulse saysFix
Developer error (10)Job 2 isn't done, or there's a typo in the package name or SHA-1. Check the Android client.
Not linked to Google Health yetJob 1: sign in to the Google Health app with the Fitbit account.
Permission refused (403)You picked the wrong account, or didn't tick every box. Sign out, then Connect again with the Fitbit account.
Access blockedThe Fitbit Gmail isn't in Test users (Phase 2, Step 4).
Sign in againThe app is still in Testing mode (7-day limit). Do the "In production" part of Job 4.
No data, empty screensOpen the Google Health app so the tracker syncs, then pull down to refresh in Pulse.
Not tested with real data yetThe app hasn't talked to the real Google Health API yet, because that needs your sign-in. Some numbers, like SpO2, skin temperature or workouts, may look different from the Google Health app at first. Tell Claude what you see, or send a screenshot, and it gets fixed.
